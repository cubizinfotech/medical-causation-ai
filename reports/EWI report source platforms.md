# Fill EWI reports from issuing bodies

Attorney-usable Expert Witness Investigation reports require **issuing-body records**, not more web search. A US personal-injury firm **cannot buy** FSMB Physician Data Center at the published **$9 Standard / $12 Premium** rates; those SKUs are sold to hospitals and credentialers, so Package B must not budget them ([FSMB PDC Query Form](https://inte.fsmb.org/PDC/pdc-query/pdc-query-form/); [FSMB PDC FAQ](https://www.fsmb.org/PDC/pdc-faq/)). The lawful license-and-discipline substitute is official state-board files and lookups (Florida, California, Texas, New York OPMC, and New Jersey rosters at **$0 list**) plus **ABMS CertiFACTS** for MD board certification (attorneys are an intended customer class; **USD is quote-only**) and, for DOs, AOA Profiles at a published **$25** only if AOIA will sell to the firm ([ABMS Solutions FAQs](https://www.abmssolutions.com/faqs/); [AOA Profiles FAQ](https://aoaprofiles.org/faq)). Reports stay thin because live mode already walks a 41-section outline while Florida MQA is the only connected board, board-certification and deposition catalog rows never send a network request, Gemini grounding returns model prose plus citation URLs rather than official HTML, and templates **3.10.6–3.10.7** identity gates that stop surname-only and court-as-credential misfile are in this workspace but **not deployed** onto historic client PDFs ([CHANGELOG](../CHANGELOG.md)). At **20 investigations per month**, keep Gemini 3 Search grounding (**5,000 free search queries/month, then $14 per 1,000**), do not apply the Gemini 2.5 Flash **1,500 RPD / $35** meter, and do not stack Serper, Brave, and Tavily ([Gemini Developer API pricing](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/pricing)). Buy PACER recap-fetch and a human TrialSmith portal before any Lexis or Westlaw seat. Package A is about **$40–$60/year**. Package B without PDC is about **$800–$1,400/year plus a CertiFACTS quote**. Package C is about **$10k/year plus quotes** and is justified only when the client wants a paralegal-grade opposing-expert file.

## Reports stay thin because issuing bodies never run

Prior EWI audits kept polishing labels, empty-state copy, and identity wording while the live path still cannot produce the records an attorney cites. `RESEARCH_PROVIDER=live` constructs ten dedicated adapters (NPI Registry, Open Payments, OpenAlex, PubMed, NIH RePORTER, ORCID, CourtListener, Justia, Florida MQA license and discipline, and YouTube when keyed) and then fills about **35 remaining catalog ids** from one shared public-web engine. The PDF looks complete because `InvestigationWorkflow` invokes every catalog id in the 41-section template **3.10.7**. Completeness of the outline is not completeness of evidence. Education verification, board certification, certification organizations, criminal records, malpractice records, LexisNexis, Crossref-as-catalog, and memberships return “live adapter is not connected” or “No request was sent.” FSMB, Westlaw, PACER recap-fetch, and TrialSmith ingest are **not in the live adapter map at all**. Setting reserved env names (`LEXISNEXIS_API_KEY`, `USPTO_API_KEY`, `GOOGLE_MAPS_API_KEY`, directory keys) does nothing.

The four holes that make a finished report feel empty are issuing-body data, undeployed identity gates, web snippets sold as official records, and human-only deposition sources. First, **licenses and board actions outside Florida never hit an issuing board**. Florida MQA runs only when the city state is FL or NPPES lists a Florida license; other states print “Official board not accessed.” NPPES license strings are self-reported and “Issuance of an NPI does not ensure or validate that the Health Care Provider is Licensed or Credentialed” ([NPPES API](https://npiregistry.cms.hhs.gov/api-page)). Board-certification catalog rows are not in the web-source list, so an own-site “board certified” sentence that 3.10.4 already refuses to treat as independent verification is the only thing that can land in that section. ABMS **Certification Matters** is free for patients and **not authorized for professional use**, so Google snippets of it are the wrong artifact ([Certification Matters FAQ](https://www.certificationmatters.org/faq/what-is-the-difference-between-a-patient-and-a-professional-user/)). Second, **templates 3.10.6 and 3.10.7 already encode the gates attorneys asked for**—surname-only pages are never “possible,” another first name with the same surname is another person, credential sections require a strong identity match, court-like pages cannot file board certification, license, education, IME, or deposition claims, CAPTCHA titles are rejected, and docket notices that mention a deposition are not transcripts—but CHANGELOG on 26 October 2026 states those reports are **not deployed** and historic client PDFs were not overwritten ([CHANGELOG](../CHANGELOG.md)). The frozen live PDF job `192fdaa2` remains template **3.9.0**. Historic Demery Word (9) on template **3.9.2** filed a Justia URL as board certification and reused an unread PACER snippet as IME, pleadings, and testimony ([capability-audit](../docs/ewi/capability-audit.md)). Audits that never regenerate those artifacts cannot change what the client holds.

Third, **web search is discovery, not verification**. Gemini grounding returns a synthesized answer plus `groundingChunks` citation metadata (often Vertex redirect URIs), not the physician’s page HTML ([Grounding with Google Search](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/google-search)). This product correctly treats that prose as a lead and fetches the URL; when the fetch hits login, CAPTCHA, robots.txt, or a paywall, the engine lists the address and does not bypass it. A `seak` live-path is public `site:seakexperts.com`, not a SEAK API. `depositions` is public mentions, not TrialSmith. `patents` is Google Patents HTML, not USPTO Open Data Portal. `google_maps` is URL classification, not Maps Platform. Attorneys reading section titles hear the named product; coverage notes try to say otherwise; a source-index full of unread PACER, Justia, Sunbiz, and LinkedIn lines still looks like a thin investigation. Fourth, **deposition transcripts have no public API**. TrialSmith is a plaintiff-lawyer membership portal: Pro members read transcripts online, downloads are **$28–$50**, and the contract forbids building a competing database or using the service if the customer represents defendants ([TrialSmith Deposition Pricing](https://www.trialsmith.com/?pg=DepositionPricing); [TrialSmith Terms](https://www.trialsmith.com/?pg=TermsAndConditions)). Lexis and Westlaw hold challenge reports, verdicts, and some transcripts, and both **ban unlicensed automation** ([LexisNexis General Terms](https://www.lexisnexis.com/en-us/terms/general/default.page)). CourtListener REST is free and already wired, but it returns published opinions plus whatever is already in RECAP, not a complete docket and not a deposition ([CourtListener REST API](https://www.courtlistener.com/help/api/rest/)).

Identity is a two-speed system that explains both empty money sections and remaining contamination. A confirmed NPI **blocks** Open Payments and CV specialty/license/payment comparison when it is missing, so section 28 can be empty for a well-known expert if intake omitted NPI or city. PubMed (last name + initials), Justia (last name in link text), NIH RePORTER, YouTube, and the entire web engine still run. Missing city on Demery intake made NPI confirmation fail the single-record city test and maximized same-name noise while web and PubMed continued. Deploying 3.10.7 plus requiring NPI and city on intake is the highest-leverage **zero-vendor** fix. It cannot invent an ABMS date or a transcript. Empty credential sections after a confirmed NPI are empty because ABMS, AOA, ABPP, and non-Florida boards are not connected.

## Do not buy PDC, dual research seats, or search stacks

FSMB’s live account-request copy opens “If you’re a **hospital or health care organization**.” The 2020 qualified-user list (Hospital, Government Agency, Credentials Verification Service, Managed Care Facility, Placement Service, Medical Group or Clinical Practice) does not include attorneys or law firms ([ACGME / FSMB statement](https://www.acgme.org/newsroom/2020/3/acgme-shares-federation-of-state-medical-boards-fsmb-information-on-access-to-physician-data-center/)). Plaintiff versus defense is irrelevant: **neither side is a named PDC buyer**. The **$9 / $12** figures are real list prices **for eligible customers** and are not a cart item for this firm. Practitioner Direct and FCVS (**$395** initial physician application) are physician-paid tools for sending *their own* credentials. DocInfo is the free consumer front end of the same database; it is not an API, FSMB’s terms forbid robots and data-mining, and unofficial DocInfo wrapper APIs are not FSMB products ([FSMB Terms of Use](https://www.fsmb.org/pdc/pdc-tos)). Email `pdc@fsmb.org` if a written eligibility answer is required; until that letter exists, public pages control the buy: **do not purchase PDC**.

NPDB is not a PI-attorney product. Defense firms cannot query it. A plaintiff’s attorney may obtain a one-time Title IV extract only in a malpractice case **against a hospital**, only after proving the hospital failed a mandatory query, and may use the data **only against the hospital, not the practitioner** ([NPDB Information for Attorneys](https://www.npdb.hrsa.gov/resources/infoForAttorneys.jsp)). Eligible-entity queries are **$2.50**; that price is irrelevant because the firm is not an eligible entity for opposing-expert work. Cheap “national practitioner” or criminal-record APIs are identity-error mills and a false “no record” risk; public web “arrested OR conviction” queries are news, not a background check.

## Conclusion

The product already knows how to look like a full investigation. What it cannot do—until Stage 0 ships and Stages 1–4 are bought as **issuing bodies and licensed humans**, not as extra SERP credits—is put a citable board file, a dated ABMS artifact, a paid PACER order, or a real transcript behind those headings.
